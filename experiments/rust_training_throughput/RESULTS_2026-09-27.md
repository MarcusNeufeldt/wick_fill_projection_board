# Initial results - 2026-09-27

## Rust parity and throughput

Both engines used the current strict wick definition and a 180-day maximum
follow-up. The parity gate compared candle rows, strict signals, direction
split, signal-index checksum, and every trace status.

| Input | Rows | Strict signals | Python | Rust | Total speedup | Trace speedup |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ETHUSDT 5m, full archive | 527,965 | 8,279 | 2.224s | 0.513s | **4.33x** | **125x** |
| ETHUSDT 1m, full archive | 2,638,312 | 63,086 | 75.337s | 2.364s | **31.87x** | **653x** |

Parity passed exactly on both full-archive runs. For the 1m archive, both
engines returned:

- 28,829 completed clean fills
- 34,176 fills before a provable departure
- 71 fully observed unfilled signals
- 10 right-censored signals

This proves that the strict-signal and clean-path tracing kernel is worth
moving to Rust. It does **not** yet prove a 32x speedup for the complete model
update, because feature materialization, entry-age snapshots, adverse-threshold
labels, Parquet output, and model fitting remain Python stages.

The second POC now covers the complete label kernel as well. Parity includes
every entry age, target touch, adverse threshold, outcome ordering, censoring
count, and lower/upper risk-envelope aggregate.

| Complete outcome kernel | Observations | Python | Parallel Rust | Speedup |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ETHUSDT 5m, fixed full prefix | 3,999 | 7.365s | 0.775s | **9.51x** |
| ETHUSDT 1m, fixed 2,638,328-row prefix | 10,075 | 127.173s | 21.901s | **5.81x** |

The first live 1m attempt correctly failed parity because two candles were
appended between sequential reads. Pinning both engines to the same row count
removed that race and produced exact parity.

The safe production bridge is now implemented: Rust exports row-level traces
and labels, while Python validates signal identity and retains the existing
schema/Parquet boundary. On a 100,000-row ETHUSDT 5m prefix, all 1,449 signal
records and 688 observation records matched Python column-for-column and
value-for-value. On the current full 527,975-row ETHUSDT 5m archive, all 8,279
signals and 3,999 observations also matched exactly. End-to-end generation was
6.203s in Python versus 3.668s through the bridge (**1.69x**), including the
remaining Python detection, feature and schema work.

The manual updater now builds the Rust release binary and invokes the outcome
builder with explicit `--engine rust`. Contract or parity failures stop that
update rather than silently publishing mismatched data. The live/frozen model
artifact was not retrained or replaced as part of this integration.

The route bridge is also implemented for all configured assets at raw 1m and
5m resolution. On a 100,000-row ETHUSDT 5m prefix, Rust and Python matched all
1,449 signal outcomes, 796 completed episodes and 221,111 full path rows. On a
100,000-row ETHUSDT 1m prefix with a two-day parity horizon, they matched all
1,724 outcomes, 912 episodes and 75,652 compact path rows. Both comparisons
matched path schemas and values exactly within floating-point tolerance. The
derived 15m compatibility routes remain in Python.

A subsequent full-size Rust-only ETHUSDT 1m library build produced 28,829
completed episodes and 20,740,487 normalized path rows. Excluding the one-time
release compilation, the library build completed in roughly 36 seconds. The
unchanged production cache builder then consumed the generated gzip, prepared
all 20.7 million states and built its 2,793,520-row `rust_native` candidate
index successfully. This verifies the new route output against the actual
downstream application contract, not only against a sample comparison.

## Fixed-window chronological learning curve

The curve used the existing `moderate` all-outcome numerical model. It kept one
fixed later query set of 1,500 observations / 856 signal episodes and trained
on nested, label-mature history ending before that query set.

| History | Episodes | 1d fill Brier | 1d adverse MAE | 7d fill Brier | 7d adverse MAE | 30d fill Brier | 30d adverse MAE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20% | 1,803 | 0.1130 | 1.030% | 0.0506 | 1.672% | **0.0305** | 2.436% |
| 40% | 3,596 | 0.1100 | 0.984% | **0.0496** | 1.574% | **0.0304** | 2.235% |
| 60% | 5,389 | 0.1101 | 0.970% | 0.0498 | 1.552% | 0.0311 | 2.210% |
| 80% | 7,195 | 0.1097 | 0.968% | 0.0504 | 1.546% | 0.0312 | 2.182% |
| 100% | 9,008 | **0.1081** | **0.957%** | 0.0498 | **1.539%** | 0.0310 | **2.175%** |

From 20% to 100% history:

- 1d fill Brier improved 4.37%; adverse MAE improved 7.08%.
- 7d fill Brier improved 1.64%; adverse MAE improved 7.92%.
- 30d fill Brier regressed 1.55%; adverse MAE improved 10.71%.
- Target-versus-2%-adverse Brier improved about 3% at all horizons.
- Target-versus-5%-adverse Brier improved 1.73% to 4.19%.

From 80% to 100%, gains were much smaller: fill Brier improved 0.61% to
1.46%, and adverse MAE improved 0.30% to 1.18%. Some wait and target-versus-2%
metrics regressed slightly.

## Interpretation

The first result rejects both extremes:

- We have **not** exhausted the value of additional episodes: adverse-magnitude
  estimates continued to improve materially as history expanded.
- More rows are **not** a universal solution: central fill and competing-event
  metrics are already showing diminishing or inconsistent gains.

The next data expansion should therefore target genuinely new adverse regimes,
long-aged signals, and additional liquid assets rather than generating more
snapshots from existing episodes. Normal and Adverse-first should remain the
two primary product routes. The next numerical research target should be the
probability, magnitude, and timing of adverse movement before fill.

This is one fixed test window and the A model, so it is development evidence,
not a promotion result for E0. A rolling multi-origin learning curve is the
next validation step.

## Three-origin learning curve

The follow-up used three non-overlapping chronological query windows with 409,
406, and 427 signal episodes. Each origin independently enforced that every
training label had matured before its first query. Values below are means over
the three origins.

| History | 1d fill | 1d risk | 7d fill | 7d risk | 7d wait | 30d fill | 30d risk | 30d wait |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20% | 0.1248 | 1.389% | **0.0748** | 2.445% | 913m | 0.0523 | 3.950% | 2,323m |
| 40% | 0.1252 | 1.357% | 0.0752 | 2.373% | 876m | 0.0526 | 3.607% | 2,249m |
| 60% | 0.1263 | 1.355% | 0.0777 | **2.324%** | 857m | 0.0519 | 3.467% | 2,204m |
| 80% | 0.1252 | **1.349%** | 0.0776 | 2.362% | 844m | 0.0519 | 3.485% | 2,186m |
| 100% | **0.1236** | 1.357% | 0.0774 | 2.359% | **837m** | **0.0516** | **3.441%** | **2,166m** |

From 20% to 100% history, mean adverse MAE improved 2.30%, 3.50%, and
12.88% at 1/7/30 days. Waiting-time MAE improved 1.61%, 8.28%, and 6.73%.
Fill Brier improved 0.99% at one day and 1.42% at 30 days, but regressed 3.44%
at seven days. Target-versus-2% and target-versus-5% Brier were essentially
flat after the middle fractions.

The 80% to 100% increment was small: roughly 0.1% to 1.3% on the metrics that
improved, with slight regressions in 1d adverse MAE and some competing-event
scores. This confirms that general data scaling is entering diminishing
returns, while long-horizon adverse magnitude remains the clearest beneficiary
of broader history.

The practical research priority is therefore not simply “more wicks.” It is
more independent examples of large adverse-first moves and mature multi-day
signals. Normal remains the central route; Adverse-first is the highest-value
specialist route to improve.
